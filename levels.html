Put and call walls are the peak open-interest strikes below and above spot. The bar runs put wall (P) to call wall (C) with spot marked in orange and max pain in grey — a spot pinned to one end is pressed against that wall, and a wide band means the pair brackets so much of the price that neither strike is a level. Walls more than 15% from spot are dimmed: at that distance the open interest is tail hedging, not a line price trades into. R:R prices the long those walls describe — bought at spot, stopped at the put wall, taking profit at the call wall — against the same 1.0 floor the blotter refuses brackets on. Most print below it, and that is the finding, not a fault in the column: the walls are set by where open interest sits, not by anything about an entry. Under each symbol: the bracket a scan actually graded today with the anchors it chose, absent for a name no scan covered — and the ATR zone, spot ∓1.5 and +2 ATR, the same anchors the grader picks from. The ATR bracket has a fixed 1.33 reward:risk by construction, so it is a reference, not a signal.
